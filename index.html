PROJECT 1

Coca-Cola, PepsiCo and Monster: who earns the best returns?

A five-year, side-by-side read of the three largest listed beverage companies. Same questions for each: how fast does it grow, how much of each dollar becomes profit and cash, how much debt does it carry, and what does it do with the cash.

What I analyzed

Income statements for FY2021 to FY2025 and balance sheets for FY2022 to FY2025 from each company's 10-K. I computed growth, margins, free cash flow, DuPont ROE, working capital days, net debt / EBITDA and shareholder payouts, and adjusted for one-off charges.

What I found

  • Monster is the growth story: 10.6% revenue and 10.7% EPS CAGR, with 29.2% operating margin and no debt.
  • Coca-Cola is the margin story: 28.7% operating margin and 41% ROE, driven more by profitability than by leverage.
  • PepsiCo is the stretched one: 12.2% margin, EPS growth of just 2.2% a year, 2.7x net debt / EBITDA, and a payout above free cash flow.

So what

Quality and growth are priced very differently across the group, which sets up Project 2: Coca-Cola and Monster trade at 28x and 41x earnings, PepsiCo at 17x. The market is paying for margin and growth, not size.

Leverage: net debt / EBITDA

Where the cash goes

PROJECT 2

Monster Beverage at $44.22: what has to go right?

A discounted cash flow valuation built on trailing twelve months to June 2026, tested against Coca-Cola and PepsiCo multiples. Move the assumptions and watch the value, sensitivity table, reverse DCF and football field update.

What I analyzed

Whether Monster's share price is supported by its cash flows. I built TTM figures (FY2025 plus the first six months of 2026 minus the first six months of 2025), forecast five years of free cash flow, discounted at a CAPM cost of equity of 7.9% (Monster has no debt), and added Monster's $4,201M of cash and investments.

What I found

  • Base case value is $30.89 per share, 30% below the $44.22 price. Bear to bull: $24 to $41.
  • Reverse DCF: the price needs about 22% annual growth for five years at a 29% margin. Revenue grew 23% in the latest six months, so it is possible, but with no room for a slowdown.
  • On multiples, Monster trades at 29x EBITDA against 27x for Coca-Cola and 12x for PepsiCo.

So what

The stock is priced for the recent pace to hold. My view (educational): the business is excellent, but at this price the margin of safety is thin. I would want either a price nearer the base case or evidence that 20%+ growth is lasting, such as international share gains.

Method notes. CAPM: risk-free rate 5.1% (10-year Treasury, mid-October 2026, quoted between 5.1% and 5.2% by different sources), equity risk premium 4.2% (Damodaran implied, Oct 1, 2026), beta 0.66 (Blume-adjusted from a 5-year monthly raw beta of 0.49). A raw beta of 0.49 would give a WACC near 7.2%, which would raise the base value; the sensitivity table covers that range. Share prices were taken from public quote pages on Oct 14–15, 2026 and the PepsiCo quote carries no timestamp, so the price is editable.

How I checked the numbers

  • Every income statement ties: revenue − cost of sales = gross profit; gross profit − operating costs = operating income, for all 15 company-years.
  • Balance sheet components never exceed their subtotals; DuPont ROE reproduces net income ÷ equity exactly.
  • Computed P/E ratios for Monster (40.9x) and Coca-Cola (27.6x) match the quote pages' own trailing P/E (40.9x and 27.6x), which validates the TTM EPS build and the split adjustment.
  • Units differ by filing (Monster in thousands, PepsiCo's FY2023 income statement in dollars) and Coca-Cola and PepsiCo use 52/53-week fiscal years; all are normalised to $ millions.

Limits: five-year DCF with a single terminal growth rate; no segment or geographic build. This is an educational project, not investment advice.
